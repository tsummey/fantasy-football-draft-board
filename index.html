Drafting Strategies

Is this board enough to get through a whole draft now?

Much closer than before. A typical draft has 10–12 teams, and each team fills a roster of roughly 15–16 total spots (starters, plus a bench of 6–9, plus often an IR spot) — 150–190+ players get drafted across the whole room. This board now carries 128 players (25 deep at RB and WR, 18 deep at QB and TE, 15 deep at FLEX and K, 12 deep at DST), so it should realistically cover you through most of a standard draft. The very last few picks of a deep league may still dip below this list, especially at RB/WR bench depth.

RB and WR still go the deepest

Most teams need 2–3+ running backs and wide receivers each (starters, plus FLEX, plus bench depth), so those two positions get drafted the fastest. The RB and WR rows go 25 deep here specifically because of that — but if you somehow burn through all 25, you're in the very final rounds and any warm body at the position is a fine, low-stakes pick.

Don't reach for K or DST early

Kicker and Defense/Special Teams are the one place where depth barely matters — the gap in scoring between the 5th-ranked kicker and the 15th-ranked kicker is small, and the same goes for defenses. Most experienced drafters wait until the very last round or two to grab these, so resist the urge to draft one "just in case" early on.

QB and TE can wait a bit too

Unless an elite option (like the Tier 1 names at the top of those rows) falls unusually far, quarterback and tight end are generally considered "streaming" or "wait" positions in a standard league — there's a steep drop-off at the very top, then a long plateau of similar value. You can often land a solid starter several rounds later than you'd expect.

How to actually use this board on draft day

Sort by Rating (high to low) before the draft starts to get a feel for the overall pecking order. During the draft, use the position filter chips to isolate whichever slot you need to fill next. As other teams draft a player — including ones you wanted — check the box in the Drafted column next to their name; the row grays out and gets crossed off so you always know who's actually still available. Turn on Hide drafted in the toolbar to shrink the list down to only real remaining options as the draft goes on. Your checkmarks are saved in this browser automatically, so refreshing the page won't lose your progress mid-draft — use Clear drafted marks once the draft is over (or before your next one) to reset the board. If your search box turns up nothing, that player likely isn't on this board at all — check a live source before passing.

Board Notes
  • Rating is a 1–10 draft-value score (10 = elite/must-start, 1 = deep speculative flier), recalculated from each player's live ADP (Average Draft Position) rank within their row every time the board is refreshed.
  • This board reflects a standard league format (team DST, not individual defensive players/IDP) — confirm your league's settings before draft day in case it's different.
  • Live data: order and ratings come from real-time, crowd-sourced PPR ADP data (12-team) via FantasyFootballCalculator's free public API, refreshed by a local script (refresh_board.py) on a daily schedule. The timestamp in the header badge shows exactly when this copy was last refreshed and how many live drafts it was built from.
  • Reasoning text is preserved from the original hand-written scouting notes for any player who was already on the board; a player who's newly climbed into a row gets a plain data note (their current ADP) instead of invented commentary, until a real note is written for them.
  • ADP shifts fast, especially close to the season — still re-check injury reports and depth charts the day of your draft.
